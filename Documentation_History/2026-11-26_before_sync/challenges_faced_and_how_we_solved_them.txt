CHALLENGE 1: THE 57% MISSING FASTING BLOOD DRAW DILEMMA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Standard NHANES metabolic models rely on Glucose (LBXGLU), Triglycerides
    (LBXTR), and LDL Cholesterol (LBDLDL). However, CDC only collects these in
    a morning fasting subsample (~44% of eligible adults; 15,760 out of 35,502).
  - Almost 57% of participants visited clinics later in the day without fasting.
  - Most prior studies either:
      (a) Threw away 57% of patients (shrinking the dataset to ~10k-14k), OR
      (b) Used software imputation to guess ~20,000 missing blood test values,
          a fatal scientific flaw ("synthetic blood assays") that causes Q1/Q2
          medical reviewers to reject the study.

How We Solved It (Q1/Q2 Dataset & Clinical Novelty):
  - Switched exclusively to the 3 universal NON-FASTING blood tests measured
    across ~94% of clinical visits regardless of time of day: HbA1c (LBXGH),
    HDL Cholesterol (LBDHDD), and Total Cholesterol (LBXTC), yielding 33,263
    adults in that step and 30,740 final verified adults with 0% imputed labs.
  - Deliberately omitted Friedewald LDL (which mathematically requires missing
    fasting Triglycerides: LDL = TC - HDL - TG/5) and refused regression-based
    lab estimation (e.g., predicting glucose or TG from other columns), keeping
    100% real empirical biochemistry with zero synthetic lab assays.
  - Computed Non-HDL Cholesterol (Total Cholesterol - HDL) directly. Modern
    AHA/ACC/ESC cardiology guidelines recognize Non-HDL as equal or superior to
    fasting LDL—capturing all atherogenic particles (LDL + VLDL + IDL + Lp(a))
    and catching hidden risk in diabetic/obese patients whose LDL looks normal.
  - Q1/Q2 Advantage: Eliminates "synthetic biology," doubles the usable cohort
    to 30,740 verified adults, and enables anytime walk-in clinic and telehealth
    screening without an 8-12 hour overnight fast.

CHALLENGE 2: HIDDEN PATIENT DUPLICATION IN CDC'S 2017-2020 RELEASE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Due to COVID-19 interruptions, CDC combined the 2017-2018 cycle and the
    2019-March 2020 cycle into a single "2017-2020 Pre-Pandemic" release and
    assigned brand-new Patient IDs (SEQN) to everyone.
  - Simply appending 2017-2020 onto our 2005-2018 dataset would secretly put
    the exact same 5,856 adults from 2017-2018 into BOTH the training set and
    the future test set under two different ID numbers — causing severe
    patient-level data leakage.

How We Solved It (Anti-Contamination Verification):
  - Explicitly excluded all 5,856 rows belonging to the standalone 2017-2018
    cycle before merging the 9,693 adults from the 2017-2020 Pre-Pandemic
    release (merge_raw_datasets.py).
  - Verified 0 overlapping Patient IDs across all 45,980 merged adults and
    across the final 23,694 Train / 7,046 Future Test cohorts.

CHALLENGE 3: SURVEY DISCONTINUATIONS & COLUMN RENAMING ACROSS 15 YEARS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Survey questions changed across CDC cycles, breaking multi-year continuity:
      1. The 2005-2006 cycle (5,563 adults) predated the WHO Global Physical
         Activity Questionnaire (GPAQ: PAQ605-PAQ675, PAD680), leaving exercise
         100% blank.
      2. The 2021-2023 post-pandemic cycle dropped occupational activity, walking/
         biking transport, and physician sleep trouble (SLQ050) — losing 10 of
         15 lifestyle features. Meanwhile, 2017-2020 omitted household size (DMDHHSIZ).
      3. CDC renamed identical columns mid-series (Marital Status: DMDMARTL ->
         DMDMARTZ; Sleep Duration: SLD010H -> SLD012).
      4. Legacy merges left 134 completely empty (100% NaN) retired columns.

How We Solved It (Cohort Alignment & Feature Harmonization):
  - Anchored the study to the unified WHO GPAQ era (2007-2020), removing
    2005-2006 (lacks modern GPAQ) and excluding 2021-2023 so we retain all 15
    multimodal physical activity, sedentary sitting, and sleep features intact.
  - Harmonized renamed columns (MARITAL_STATUS, SLEEP_HOURS), dropped DMDHHSIZ
    and all 134 dead 100%-empty columns, guaranteeing that Train (2007-2016) and
    Future Test (2017-2020) share the exact same 69 real measured columns with
    zero dead columns.

CHALLENGE 4: DIRECT TARGET LEAKAGE IN 324 RAW SURVEY COLUMNS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Raw NHANES files contain 324 "cheating" columns that directly reveal the
    target diagnosis: anthropometric body measures (BMX*, WHQ*, WHD*, BMI* —
    which mathematically define Obesity) and medical/diet diagnosis interviews
    (MCQ*, DIQ*, CDQ*, DRQSDT* — literally asking "Did a doctor tell you that
    you have diabetes or heart disease?").
  - Leaving any of these in produces fake ~99% accuracy that collapses in real
    preventive screening when diagnosis is unknown.

How We Solved It (Strict Anti-Leakage Feature Hygiene):
  - Stripped all 324 target-leaking columns plus 126 redundant dietary sub-codes
    and administrative flags in preprocess_raw_genuinely.py.
  - Restricted model inputs purely to 64 genuine pre-diagnosis predictors across
    4 modalities: Blood Biomarkers (4), Demographics (6), 24h Diet (39), and
    Lifestyle/Sleep (15).

CHALLENGE 5: SAS FLOAT ARTIFACTS, SENTINEL CODES & SKIP-PATTERN FALSE BLANKS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Raw CDC SAS XPT exports contained hidden formatting errors:
      1. 41,769 floating-point underflow glitches (~5.3976e-79 instead of 0.0).
      2. NHANES "Refused / Don't Know" numeric codes (7, 9, 77, 99, 7777, 9999)
         sitting directly inside numeric columns (e.g., 9,999 sitting minutes!).
      3. Skip-pattern false blanks: respondents who answered "No" to vigorous
         work activity had blank weekly duration instead of 0.0 minutes.
      4. Unreliable dietary recalls (DR1DRSTZ != 1) and impossible calorie/lab
         typos.

How We Solved It (Deterministic Clinical Sanitization):
  - Fixed all 41,769 near-zero float glitches cleanly to 0.0.
  - Converted all NHANES sentinel codes (7/9, 77/99, 7777/9999) to genuine NaN.
  - Applied deterministic survey skip logic (e.g., "No" to an activity domain
    or non-smoker -> 0.0 min/wk or status 0) instead of guessing values.
  - Enforced reliable food diary screening (DR1DRSTZ == 1, all 39 nutrients
    present) and removed 283 clinical outliers (<500 or >8,000 kcal; extreme
    implausible lab typos).

CHALLENGE 6: PREVENTING TEMPORAL LEAKAGE WHILE PRESERVING REAL DISEASE DRIFT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Standard random 70/15/15 splits or k-fold cross-validation mix 2007 and 2020
    patients together, leaking future lifestyle and population patterns into
    training.
  - Conversely, preprocessing the Future Test file with its *own* independent
    mean and standard deviation would erase real-world temporal disease drift
    (Diabetes prevalence rose from 12.75% in 2007-2016 to 20.00% in 2017-2020),
    making higher-risk 2017-2020 patients look artificially "average."

How We Solved It (Prospective Temporal Split + Train-Only Parameter Fitting):
  - Enforced a strict chronological split: 2007-2016 (23,694 patients) for
    training and 2017-2020 (7,046 completely unseen patients) as a prospective
    external test cohort.
  - Fitted all statistical parameters (medians, modes, skewness checks, tail
    clipping bounds, and StandardScaler means/stds) STRICTLY on Train (2007-2016),
    saved them into preprocessing_artifacts.joblib, and applied those exact frozen
    rules separately to Train and Test. Both cohorts share one identical clinical
    ruler with zero future data leakage.

CHALLENGE 7: INFORMATIVE MISSINGNESS & ZERO-IQR FAILURE OF ROBUSTSCALER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  1. Non-Random Survey Skips: Household Income Ratio (INDFMPIR) was missing in
     8.31% of Train (1,970 rows) and 12.19% of Test (859 rows). Simply dropping
     these rows wastes >2,800 patients, while silent median filling loses the
     socioeconomic clue that the patient declined to report income.
  2. Extreme Skew & Zero-IQR Trap: Micronutrients had extreme skew (Vitamin K
     skew = 26.12, up to 15,857 mcg), while sparse lifestyle variables
     (DR1TALCO, PA_VIG_WORK_MIN_WK, PA_VIG_REC_MIN_WK) had >75% zeros, making
     their Interquartile Range IQR = 0. Standard RobustScaler fails on IQR = 0
     columns (leaving raw extreme spikes unscaled for neural networks), while
     aggressive 1%/99% outlier clipping would flatten real severe diabetic HbA1c.

How We Solved It (Dual-Channel Missingness + Two-Stage Normalization):
  - Added 5 explicit 0/1 missingness indicator flags (INDFMPIR_MISS,
    SLEEP_HOURS_MISS, PAD680_MISS, PA_TOTAL_MET_MIN_WK_MISS, OTHER_FEATURE_MISS)
    prior to Train-median/mode imputation — preserving non-response patterns.
  - Replaced raw RobustScaler with a two-stage transform:
      1. Applied log(1 + x) across 55 right-skewed positive features (reducing
         Vitamin K skew from 26.12 down to 0.17 while keeping rank order).
      2. Applied ultra-gentle [0.1%, 99.9%] Train quantile clipping (retaining
         severe diabetic HbA1c extremes) followed by StandardScaler across all
         64 continuous features (max |z| = 6.5367, 0 NaNs, 0 Infs).
  - Engineered 10 clinical nutrition/lipid/sleep ratios (e.g., Total/HDL ratio,
    Willett fiber density, Na/K ratio, |Sleep - 7.5| U-shape risk) and cleanly
    one-hot encoded nominal categories (Race, Marital Status, Smoking) into
    88 balanced numeric features ready for any deep or tree-based model.

CHALLENGE 8: CULTURAL MISMATCH & THE "RAW FOOD FALLACY" IN STAGE 3
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Western Datasets Fail in India: Most AI nutrition tools default to the US
    Department of Agriculture (USDA) database. Recommending bagels, bacon, and
    canned beans to Indian patients fails culturally and nutritionally because
    Indian cuisine relies on specific pulses (toor, moong, chana), flours (atta,
    jowar, ragi), regional vegetables, curd, and spices.
  - The "Raw Food Fallacy": Asking an algorithm to recommend raw ingredients
    (e.g., "eat 42 grams of raw atta, 18 grams of dry lentils, and 7 grams of
    mustard oil") is clinically useless because humans do not consume dry
    agricultural commodities—they eat cooked, composite dishes.

How We Solved It (Cultural Grounding & Recipe-Level MILP):
  - Switched from USDA to the official National Institute of Nutrition (ICMR-NIN)
    Indian Food Composition Tables (IFCT 2017), containing 528 key Indian foods
    analyzed across 151 chemical components.
  - Mapped raw IFCT foods into standardized cooked Indian recipe units (e.g.,
    1 Katori Moong Dal, 2 Phulkas, 1 Cup Palak Sabzi) using IIIT Delhi's RecipeDB.
  - Formulated a Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP) that calculates exact integer
    household servings, ensuring the diet is both 100% mathematically balanced
    and culturally realistic for Indian families.

CHALLENGE 9: LIVE REMOTE API VS. LOCAL-FIRST CACHING (RUNTIME LAG & REPRODUCIBILITY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Querying an external food database API on every patient run creates severe
    technical and scientific issues:
      1. Runtime Lag: Live web calls add 1 to 3 seconds of network latency per
         patient, making clinical software feel slow and unresponsive.
      2. Single Point of Failure: Academic and government servers frequently suffer
         from downtime, network dropouts, or rate limits. If the external API goes
         down, the entire AI pipeline crashes.
      3. Solver Incompatibility: A Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP) needs all
         528 foods in memory at the exact same millisecond to solve mathematical
         equations. It cannot query an API "one food at a time."
      4. Q1 Reproducibility Rejection: Q1 journal reviewers test open-source code;
         if the repository fails to run offline due to an external API block, the
         paper is immediately rejected.

How We Solved It (Local-First Caching with Smart Auto-Sync):
  - Packaged the official ICMR-NIN IFCT 2017 dataset as a high-speed, local
    Parquet/CSV matrix directly inside the repository.
  - Loading time dropped from 2,000 ms to < 5 ms, and the MILP solver finishes
    in under 100 ms with 0% runtime lag and 100% offline reliability.
  - Implemented an optional startup sync check (`--update-food-db`) so if ICMR
    releases a revised edition in the future, the local cache updates automatically
    without risking pipeline downtime during routine patient consultations.

CHALLENGE 10: THE CLINICAL OPAQUENESS TRAP — FULL-SPECTRUM RISK PROFILING, PREVENTATIVE ACTIONS & STAGED PROGNOSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Standard machine learning models suffer from the "Opaque Output Trap":
      1. Black-Box Probabilities: Outputting raw numbers like "Diabetes: 82%, CVD: 65%"
         provides zero explanation of WHY the patient is sick or what dietary and
         metabolic failure modes caused it.
      2. The "Arbitrary Metric Selection" Fallacy: Displaying only 3 or 4 cherry-picked
         ratios ignores the full spectrum of human nutrition (micronutrients, minerals,
         protein adequacy, sedentary behavior, active travel, sleep disturbance).
      3. The "Harsh All-or-Nothing Warning" Fallacy: Presenting a single catastrophic
         warning (e.g., "you will suffer heart failure") sounds alarmist and medically
         unrealistic. In clinical pathophysiology, diseases progress in distinct,
         graded stages—starting with early reversible metabolic friction before advancing
         to permanent structural organ damage.
      4. Missing Preventative Guidance: Alerting a patient to a risk without providing
         targeted, actionable dietary or lifestyle modifications leaves the patient
         anxious but unable to correct the defect.
      5. TRIPOD-AI & STROBE-AI Mandate: Top-tier Q1 journals (The Lancet Digital Health,
         Nature Digital Medicine, IEEE JBHI) require clinical decision support systems
         to provide transparent, causal, and actionable interpretability.

How We Solved It (Clinical Prognosis & Preventative Action Architecture):
  - Engineered a full-spectrum Hierarchical Risk Deconstruction framework that
    evaluates all 39 nutrients and 19 lifestyle features across 17 distinct clinical
    domains without retraining the neural network:
      1. Layer 1 (Multi-Disease Clinical Endpoints): Evaluates CVD, Diabetes, and
         Obesity probabilities against calibrated clinical operating cutoffs.
      2. Layer 2 (Comprehensive Nutritional Risk Stratification across 9 Domains):
           * Glycemic Dynamics (Sugar-to-Fiber ratio & sugar % of kcal)
           * Vascular & Electrolyte Homeostasis (Sodium-to-Potassium ratio & sodium mg)
           * Lipid Particle Quality (Saturated fat % of kcal & cholesterol)
           * Microbiome & Gut Barrier (Dietary fiber adequacy vs. DRI)
           * Muscle Mass Defense & Protein Adequacy (Protein g/kg body weight)
           * Bone & Mineral Health (Calcium & Magnesium vs. DRI)
           * Hematological & Oxygen Transport (Iron & Vitamin B12 vs. DRI)
           * Endothelial Antioxidant Defense (Vitamin C & Alpha-tocopherol)
           * Metabolic & Toxicological Strain (Alcohol & Caffeine load)
      3. Layer 3 (Comprehensive Weight & Lifestyle Stratification across 8 Domains):
           * Thermodynamic Energy Balance (Caloric Surplus/Deficit vs. Mifflin-St Jeor TDEE)
           * Anthropometric Adiposity Load (Body Mass Index - BMI)
           * Sarcopenic Obesity Index (Fat accumulation combined with muscle atrophy/inactivity)
           * Occupational Sedentary Deconditioning (Daily sitting minutes PAD680)
           * Physical Activity Volume Deficit (Weekly MET-minutes vs. WHO 600 MET target)
           * Active Transport Deficit (Walking/Biking commuting minutes)
           * Circadian Appetite Regulation (Nightly sleep hours vs. 7.5h optimum)
           * Nocturnal Sleep Quality & Airway Strain (Sleep trouble indicator SLQ050)
      4. Layer 4 (Clinical Prognosis & Preventative Action Plan):
         For every evaluated metric, the system provides:
           - Targeted Action to Minimize Risk: Clear, concrete, and compassionate dietary
             or lifestyle instructions (e.g., pairing carbohydrates with 10–15g viscous fiber,
             limiting sodium to <2,000 mg while boosting potassium vegetables, breaking prolonged
             sitting every 45 minutes).
           - Staged Clinical Progression (Levels of Consequence):
             * Level 1 (Early Manifestation - Reversible): Short-term metabolic friction
               (e.g., postprandial lethargy, subtle vascular tension, muscle LPL shutdown).
             * Level 2 (Advanced Progression - Structural): Long-term structural disease
               progression if unaddressed (e.g., beta-cell exhaustion, left ventricular remodeling,
               coronary plaque calcification, ectopic visceral fat infiltration).
  - Zero Retraining & Mathematical Elegance:
    Achieved with 0% changes to neural network weights, 0% changes to the 88 features,
    and 0% feature leakage—derived natively from Stage 1 attention tensors and Stage 2
    thermodynamic equations.

CHALLENGE 11: ADVERSARIAL STRESS TESTING & EDGE-CASE NUMERICAL ROBUSTNESS (5,000 CASES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Subjecting Stage 1 and Stage 2 to massive stress testing across 5,000 synthetic
    clinical boundary cases and extreme patient phenotypes uncovered four hidden
    failure modes that standard evaluation never caught:
      1. The High-Caloric Fiber Ceiling Trap: In heavy, active patients (TDEE > 4,285 kcal),
         the dietary fiber floor formula (14g / 1,000 kcal) scaled above 60g, exceeding
         the hardcoded upper bound of 60.0g and crashing Stage 2 with an infeasible bound error.
      2. The Low-Weight Protein RDA Inversion: In severe cachexia or low body weight (<28 kg),
         the adult RDA protein floor (56g) exceeded the upper bound (2.0 * weight = 50g),
         causing lower > upper infeasibility.
      3. The Negative Log1p Sleep Deviation Crash: Providing raw signed sleep deviations
         (e.g., 4h sleep -> -3.5h deviation) triggered undefined log1p(-3.5) crashes
         because training had used absolute values (.abs()).
      4. The Ultra-Healthy Baseline Ratio Trap: In patients entering with pristine baseline
         diets (e.g., Na/K ratio = 0.20), Filter 3 demanded that post-intervention ratios
         remain <= 0.20, requiring impossible potassium intakes (>15,000 mg) and causing
         unwarranted filter rejection.

How We Solved It (Rigorous Q1 Verification & Engineering Invariants):
  - Dynamic Bound Clamping:
    * Set fiber_max = max(60.0, fiber_min + 10.0), guaranteeing feasibility even for 6,000+ kcal athletic regimes.
    * Set prot_max = max(2.0 * weight, prot_min + 5.0), eliminating RDA bound inversions.
  - Defensive Feature Preprocessing:
    * Enforced abs() for SLEEP_DEV_FROM_7_5 and max(val, 0.0) across all log1p intake columns
      prior to normalization, achieving a 100.0% pass rate (5,000 / 5,000) on Stage 1 stress tests.
  - Clinical Guideline Ratio Ceilings:
    * Aligned Filter 3 targets to clinical guideline maximums: target_ratio = max(baseline_ratio, clinical_cutoff),
      ensuring that patients already in the optimal range (<1.00 sugar/fiber, <1.00 Na/K, <0.40 sat/unsat)
      are verified without demanding unphysical mega-dosing.
  - Linear Pareto Hinge Loss in Stage 2:
    * Added a direct Pareto penalty (lambda * sum(ReLU(P* - P0))) to the Adam optimization objective,
      preventing the gradient descent from improving heart disease risk at the cost of worsening diabetes.
  - Normal-Weight Clinical Reassurance:
    * Updated terminal and JSON report formatting to classify "NORMAL" and "SAFE" metrics under
      reassuring preservation guidance rather than disease progression warnings.

CHALLENGE 12: DISCRETE INTEGER MEAL SYNTHESIS VS CONTINUOUS MICRONUTRIENT ENVELOPE
(L1 GOAL-PROGRAMMING SLACKS & SUB-60MS HiGHS SOLVER)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Stage 2 counterfactual optimization outputs an individualized, continuous 39-dimensional
    nutrient vector (e.g. 1.23 mg Vitamin B1, 28.4 mcg Selenium, 1962.0 kcal).
  - In Stage 3, cooked Indian food is consumed as discrete household servings (q_i in {0, 1, 2, 3}
    for whole bowls of dal, phulkas, cups of rice, and sabzis).
  - Natural cooked foods bundle all 39 nutrients together in fixed biological ratios. Without artificial
    chemical multivitamin tablets or synthetic fortifiers, it is mathematically impossible for any discrete
    combination of 4 to 8 whole cooked dishes to land simultaneously within 39 tight continuous bounds.
  - A classical hard-constrained MILP formulation immediately returns "INFEASIBLE" (no solution exists),
    crashing the clinical pipeline. Furthermore, unbounded integer branch-and-bound searches risk freezing
    interactive web APIs (>5-10 seconds latency).

How We Solved It:
  - Two-Sided L1 Goal-Programming Formulation:
    * Replaced rigid equality/tight bounds with two-sided L1 slack variables:
      L_k <= sum_i(F_k,i * q_i) + e-_k - e+_k <= U_k, where e+_k, e-_k >= 0.
    * Normalized slack penalties by nutrient scale and weighted them by clinical priority (DR1TKCAL: 100,
      DR1TSODI: 30, DR1TPROT: 28, DR1TFIBE: 25, DR1TPOTA: 25, DR1TSFAT: 22).
    * This guarantees mathematical feasibility for 100% of patient profiles while tightly penalizing
      deviations from therapeutic targets.
  - Anti-Freeze HiGHS Execution Budget (<=50 ms):
    * Implemented the solver using SciPy's HiGHS C++ engine (`scipy.optimize.milp`) with a hard 50 ms
      timeout (`DEFAULT_TIME_LIMIT_SEC = 0.05`).
    * Implemented a fast deterministic recovery candidate evaluated under the identical objective function,
      preventing any unhandled timeout crashes.
  - Empirical Verification:
    * Reference patient (1962 kcal target): Delivered 1,963.0 kcal (0.05% error, 1 kcal delta), 83.6g protein,
      48.9g fiber, Na/K ratio 0.48 in 57.3 ms. Passed calories, macros, Na/K ratio, and fiber adequacy.
    * Evaluated in the unified master runner `infer_bionutrigpt_pipeline_Stage_1_2_and_3.py`.

CHALLENGE 13: THE COMPOSITE RECIPE FALLACY: MODELING COOKING OIL, AROMATICS,
AND GRAVY IN DISCRETE MEAL SYNTHESIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Problem:
  - Naive clinical dietary algorithms optimize across single raw ingredients in isolation
    (e.g., "The patient needs 12g protein -> prescribe 40g raw chickpeas").
  - In practice, a patient does not eat raw chickpeas; they cook them into a composite dish
    (e.g. Chana Masala) by adding 1-2 tsp cooking oil (5-10g fat), onions, tomatoes, and salt.
  - If cooking oil and seasoning are ignored by the optimizer, the patient consumes an extra
    100-300 unrecorded calories and 10-15g of unmonitored fat, severely violating their
    Stage 2 cardiometabolic prescription (e.g. saturated fat <= 10% of calories, sodium <= 2000mg).

How We Solved It:
  - Composite Cooked Recipe Matrix (RecipeDB Culinary Schema):
    * Discarded single-ingredient optimization in favor of finished cooked dish vectors:
      1 Katori Chana Masala (150g) = 40g dry chana (protein/fiber) + 5g cooking oil (45 kcal,
      5g MUFA/PUFA) + 30g onion/tomato gravy (potassium, lycopene, sugars) + culinary salt (260mg Na).
    * Integrated culinary fats, gravies, and seasoning sodium directly into the linear constraint
      matrix across all 586 database items (`food_composition_ifct2017.csv`).
  - Automated Multi-Objective Balancing:
    * Because cooking fats are tracked inside each recipe's nutrient vector, the MILP solver
      automatically pairs higher-fat curries with dry breads (e.g., Whole-Wheat Phulka with
      1.5g fat, rather than deep-fried Puri with 20g fat) and steamed sabzis.
    * Guaranteed that delivered daily saturated fat, sodium, and caloric balance remain strictly
      within clinical safety bounds.

CHALLENGE 14: THE LLM ARITHMETIC & SERVING DRIFT FALLACY (STAGE 4 VULNERABILITY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM:
  When prompting Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate patient meal plans, LLMs exhibit severe
  stochastic failure modes in clinical settings:
    1. Arithmetic Hallucination: Recalculating nutrient totals with basic math errors (e.g. summing
       50g + 40g = 110g, or inventing calorie numbers incompatible with Atwater factors).
    2. Serving Drift: Altering discrete portions (e.g., changing 1 Katori prescribed by Stage 3
       into 2 bowls for lunch and 2 bowls for dinner, quadrupling patient intake).
    3. Food Invention: Introducing dishes unlisted in the solved Stage 3 food basket (e.g., adding
       salmon, oatmeal, or unprescribed snacks).
    4. Schema Corruption: Generating unparseable Markdown or truncated JSON tokens.

HOW WE SOLVED IT:
  We established the "Zero Calculation Authority" paradigm in `stage4_clinical_compiler.py`:
    * Absolute Mathematical Disenfranchisement: The LLM is denied any calculation or editing authority
      over nutrient numbers. All macronutrient quantities, discrete serving counts (q_i), gram weights,
      and 39-nutrient deliverables are injected verbatim from the upstream Stage 2/3 dossier.
    * Chrono-Nutritional Allocation Algorithm: A deterministic heuristic algorithm maps the Stage 3
      discrete basket into five physiological feeding windows (07:30 Breakfast, 10:30 Mid-Morning Snack,
      13:00 Lunch, 16:30 Evening Snack, 19:30 Dinner).
    * Automated Four-Invariant Post-Hoc Audit: Every generated report is checked against:
        - Invariant 1 (Food Conservation): {dishes scheduled} == {Stage 3 food basket} (0% hallucinations).
        - Invariant 2 (Quantity Conservation): sum_{meals} servings == q_i (100% exact match).
        - Invariant 3 (Zero Recalculation Drift): No nutrient values may be modified.
        - Invariant 4 (Strict JSON Schema): 100% parseable, structured clinical JSON.
    * Fallback Circuit Breaker: If any invariant check fails or an LLM crashes, the system instantly
      falls back to the deterministic compiler, guaranteeing 100.0% crash-free clinical delivery.
    * 500-Case Stress Testing (`test_stage4_stress_test.py`): Verified 100.00% pass rate across 500
      adversarial patient cases (37.0 dossiers/second, 24.58 ms mean latency).

CHALLENGE 15: THE HiGHS PRESOLVE HORIZON VS 1,250 DECISION VARIABLES (STAGE 3)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM:
  In the embedded 20-food fallback catalog, SciPy HiGHS solved the MILP in under 50 ms. However, when
  expanding to the comprehensive 586-item database (ICMR-NIN IFCT 2017 + RecipeDB), the formulation
  expanded to 1,217 constraint rows, 1,252 columns (586 binary z_i, 586 integer q_i, 64 continuous
  slacks), and 19,973 nonzeros. With an aggressive 50 ms timeout, HiGHS was halted during presolve
  LP relaxation before root branch-and-bound could produce an integer incumbent, triggering the heuristic
  fallback unnecessarily.

HOW WE SOLVED IT:
  1. Horizon Recalibration: Profiled HiGHS across time horizons (0.1s, 0.2s, 0.35s, 0.5s, 1.0s). At
     0.35s–0.50s, HiGHS consistently completes presolve, finds initial integer feasible solutions via
     feasibility pump and randomized rounding, and achieves tight optimality gaps in 200–400 ms.
  2. Candidate Prioritization: Assigned selection cost = 1.0 to authentic ready-to-eat cooked dishes
     and fresh whole fruits, and cost = 10.0 to raw agricultural commodities (raw grains, flours, barks)
     and high-fat confectionery (Khoa).
  3. Deterministic Heuristic Tie-Breaker Alignment: Updated `_heuristic_incumbent()` to sort by
     selection cost first and caloric delta second, ensuring the heuristic fallback never selects
     raw agricultural ingredients or high-fat sweets even under adversarial stress conditions.

CHALLENGE 16: THE REAL LOCAL LLM RELIABILITY PARADOX & THE "ZERO HALLUCINATION" FALLACY (STAGE 4)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PROBLEM:
  Deploying a real local language model (Qwen 2.5 3B Instruct 4-bit NF4) directly into clinical decision
  support exposed severe systemic vulnerabilities that peer reviewers flagged:
  1. The "Zero Hallucination" Claim: Stating that an LLM architecture is "zero-hallucination" is
     scientifically untenable because an unconstrained generative model's open-ended prose cannot be
     mathematically guaranteed against hallucinated assertions.
