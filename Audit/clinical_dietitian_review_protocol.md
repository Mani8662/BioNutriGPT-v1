# BioNutriGPT Clinical Multi-Rater Evaluation Protocol
## Blinded Expert Evaluation of Algorithmic Indian Meal Plans

### 1. Purpose and Clinical Boundary
This protocol defines a rigorous, prospective evaluation framework designed to assess the **nutritional adequacy, cultural acceptability, and volumetric feasibility** of dietary plans generated by the BioNutriGPT hybrid optimization pipeline. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Clinical Boundary Statement:**
> Algorithmic meal planning and counterfactual rescoring establish **algorithmic face validity and mathematical feasibility only**. They **do not** constitute clinical evidence of causal health improvement or therapeutic efficacy. Clinical effectiveness must be demonstrated through future prospective randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

---

### 2. Panel of Clinical Evaluators
- **Panel Composition:** Three independent Registered Dietitians (RDs) certified by the Indian Dietetic Association (IDA) with $\ge 5$ years of outpatient clinical nutrition experience managing cardiometabolic diseases (type 2 diabetes, dyslipidemia, obesity, and hypertension).
- **Blinding Procedure:** Triple-blinded evaluation.
  - The 50 generated meal plans are presented in identical typographical formats alongside baseline comparative plans (Linear Programming relaxation, standard clinical guideline templates, and BioNutriGPT discrete MILP).
  - Evaluators are blinded to the algorithm/origin of each plan, the model architecture, and the ratings of other evaluators.
  - Order of presentation is randomized using a cryptographically secure random permutation.

---

### 3. Evaluation Dimensions (5-Point Likert Scales)

Each meal plan is independently scored across five prespecified clinical dimensions:

| Dimension | Description | Clinical Criteria for Top Score (5/5) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Volumetric Feasibility** | Realism of portion sizes and total daily meal volume. | Portions correspond strictly to discrete standard Indian household measures (e.g., integer katoris, whole phulkas). Meal volumes are physiologically realistic for consumption across 3 main meals and 2 snacks without gastrointestinal distress. |
| **2. Cultural & Regional Palatability** | Authenticity of Indian culinary pairings and combinations. | Flavor pairings follow traditional culinary coherence (e.g., dal paired with phulka/rice, appropriate sabzi textures). Zero incongruous food combinations or culturally unacceptable culinary mandates. |
| **3. Nutritional Adequacy** | Alignment with ICMR-NIN 2020 Recommended Dietary Allowances (RDA). | Macronutrient distribution complies with cardiometabolic goals (45–55% complex carbohydrate, 15–20% protein, <7% saturated fat, $\ge 35\text{ g}$ dietary fiber). Micronutrient thresholds (iron, zinc, folate, calcium, potassium) are achieved without synthetic fortification. |
| **4. Glycemic & Chrono-Sequencing** | Temporal distribution of glycemic load across the circadian day. | Complex carbohydrates and high-fiber lentils are prioritized during active midday hours (lunch); dinners are light with controlled carbohydrate density; snacks provide sustained satiety via protein/fiber rather than refined simple carbohydrates. |
| **5. Clinical Safety & Invariant Adherence** | Absence of contraindications and compliance with strict clinical safety bounds. | Strict compliance with disease-specific safety limits: Sodium $\le 1,500\text{--}2,000\text{ mg/day}$ for hypertension; potassium guidelines strictly differentiate CVD/hypertension (DASH promotes potassium) from CKD (where hyperkalemia requires potassium restriction). Zero allergens or contraindicated foods. |

---

### 4. Scoring Rubric

Each dimension is scored from 1 to 5:
- **1 - Unacceptable:** Severe clinical or culinary flaws; completely unviable for patient implementation.
- **2 - Poor:** Substantial violations of portion feasibility, culinary coherence, or nutritional targets.
- **3 - Acceptable:** Clinically safe and generally feasible, but contains minor portion anomalies or suboptimal culinary pairings.
- **4 - Good:** Highly appropriate nutritional distribution and portioning with realistic household measures.
- **5 - Excellent:** Publication-grade clinical dietetics standard; optimal culinary synergy, precise nutrient alignment, and seamless home preparation feasibility.

---

### 5. Statistical Analysis of Inter-Rater Reliability
To quantify agreement across the three independent clinical dietitians without assuming numerical equidistance:
1. **Krippendorff's Alpha ($\alpha$)**: Calculated on ordinal data across all 5 dimensions. Target: $\alpha \ge 0.70$ indicating strong inter-rater consensus.
2. **Fleiss' Generalized Kappa ($\kappa$)**: Calculated across dichotomized feasibility ratings (Acceptable $\ge 4$ vs Unacceptable $< 4$). Target: $\kappa \ge 0.65$.
3. **Friedman Rank-Sum Test**: Non-parametric paired comparison across paradigms (Discrete MILP vs Relaxed LP vs Unconstrained LLM vs Guideline Baseline) followed by post-hoc Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Bonferroni correction ($\alpha = 0.05$).

---

### 6. Ethical and Institutional Approvals
All patient archetypes are fully anonymized clinical profiles synthesized from NHANES epidemiological distributions mapped to ICMR-NIN RDA parameters. No direct human subject interventional procedures are performed during this in silico validation phase. Prospective patient intervention trials will be submitted under separate Institutional Review Board (IRB) and Clinical Trials Registry - India (CTRI) oversight prior to clinical deployment.
