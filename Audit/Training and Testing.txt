1. COHORT DESIGN & TEMPORAL DATA PARTITIONING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A fundamental flaw in existing biomedical machine learning literature is random
cross-validation (e.g., standard 80/20 k-fold splits), which randomly mixes 
patients across historical survey waves and causes severe optimistic temporal 
leakage. BioNutriGPT enforces a strict, prospective temporal partition:

1. Training & Internal Validation Cohort:
   - Source: CDC NHANES continuous survey cycles 2007-2008, 2009-2010, 
     2011-2012, 2013-2014, and 2015-2016.
   - Total Verified Cohort Size: N = 23,694 adult participants (aged >= 18).
   - Internal Partition: Stratified split into 20,140 training rows (85%) 
     and 3,554 validation rows (15%).
   - Function: Model parameter optimization, learning rate scheduling, and early 
     stopping selection.

2. Prospective External Test Cohort (Strict Holdout):
   - Source: CDC NHANES 2017-March 2020 Pre-Pandemic cycle.
   - Total Verified Cohort Size: N = 7,046 adult participants.
   - Protocol: Completely untouched during all data cleaning, imputation fitting, 
     feature transformation, and neural architecture tuning. Evaluated only once 
     after weights were permanently frozen.
   - Temporal Integrity: Evaluates whether a model trained on pre-2016 historical 
     data accurately generalizes to future cohorts.

3. Systematic Patient Deduplication:
   - All overlapping sequence numbers (SEQN) between the legacy 2017-2018 cycle 
     and the merged 2017-March 2020 pre-pandemic dataset were systematically 
     cross-referenced and deduplicated to ensure exactly 0% patient leakage.

2. INPUT FEATURE SPACE & PREPROCESSING PROTOCOL (88 FEATURES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The model ingests 88 clean features partitioned into four physiological streams:

- Stream 1: Blood Biomarkers (6 features)
  * Raw: HbA1c (LBXGH), HDL-C (LBDHDD), Total Cholesterol (LBXTC), 
         Derived Non-HDL Cholesterol (NON_HDL_CHOL = LBXTC - LBDHDD).
  * Derived Ratios: Total Cholesterol / HDL (CHOL_HDL_RATIO),
                    Non-HDL / HDL (NON_HDL_TO_HDL_RATIO).
  * Integrity: 100% genuine laboratory measurements. Fasting morning-only labs 
    (fasting glucose, insulin, triglycerides) were excluded to maintain a 
    realistic walk-in outpatient setting without synthetic imputation.

- Stream 2: 24-Hour Nutrition & Derived Ratios (46 features)
  * 39 Curated Nutrients: Calories, Protein, Carbohydrates, Total Sugars, 
    Dietary Fiber, Total Fat, Saturated Fat, Monounsaturated Fat, Polyunsaturated 
    Fat, Cholesterol, 11 Vitamins (A, B1, B2, B6, B12, C, E, K, Folate), 
    9 Minerals (Calcium, Phosphorus, Magnesium, Iron, Zinc, Copper, Sodium, 
    Potassium, Selenium), Caffeine, Alcohol.
  * 7 Clinical Ratios: Protein % kcal, Carbohydrate % kcal, Fat % kcal, 
    Fiber per 1,000 kcal, Sugar-to-Fiber ratio, Sodium-to-Potassium ratio, 
    Saturated-to-Unsaturated Fat ratio.

- Stream 3: Lifestyle, Physical Activity & Sleep (19 features)
  * Sleep: Total sleep hours, deviation from 7.5 hours (|sleep - 7.5|), 
    physician-diagnosed sleep trouble flag (SLQ050).
  * Physical Activity: Daily sedentary sitting minutes (PAD680), 5 WHO GPAQ 
    domains (Vigorous work, Moderate work, Walking/biking transport, Vigorous 
    recreation, Moderate recreation), total weekly MET-minutes, 5 GPAQ screening flags.
  * Tobacco Exposure: 4 one-hot smoking categories (Never, Former, Some Days, 
    Every Day).

- Stream 4: Demographics & Missingness Indicators (17 features)
  * Continuous/Ordinal: Age (RIDAGEYR), Poverty-Income Ratio (INDFMPIR), 
    Education level (DMDEDUC2).
  * Categorical/One-Hot: Female gender flag, Race/Ethnicity (5 categories), 
    Marital status (3 categories).
  * Informative Missingness Flags: INDFMPIR_MISS, SLEEP_HOURS_MISS, PAD680_MISS, 
    PA_TOTAL_MET_MIN_WK_MISS, OTHER_FEATURE_MISS.

CRITICAL ANTI-LEAKAGE DESIGN RULE:
  All anthropometric physical measures (BMXBMI / BMI, BMXWT / Weight, 
  BMXHT / Height, BMXWAIST / Waist Circumference) were strictly BANNED 
  from input features. Including body weight or BMI when predicting obesity 
  causes circular target leakage. Stage 1 predicts adiposity risk purely from 
  metabolism, energy balance, and lifestyle.

Preprocessing Pipeline (preprocessing_artifacts.joblib):
  1. Train-Only Fitting: All scaling parameters, medians, modes, and clipping 
     bounds were derived strictly from the 2007-2016 training set.
  2. Log1p Transformation: Applied to 55 continuous features with skewness > 1.0:
     x_log = log(1 + max(x, 0))
  3. Quantile Clipping: Extreme outlier suppression bounded between the 0.1% 
     and 99.9% training quantiles [Q_0.001, Q_0.999].
  4. Standard Scaling: Zero-mean and unit-variance standardization:
     z = (x_clipped - mean_train) / std_train.

3. MULTI-TASK TARGET LABELS & IMBALANCE HANDLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Stage 1 simultaneously predicts three major chronic cardiometabolic outcomes:

1. Type 2 Diabetes (TARGET_DIABETES):
   - Definition: Lab HbA1c >= 6.5% OR self-reported physician diagnosis.
   - Train Prevalence: 3,022 / 23,694 (12.8% positive, 87.2% negative).
   - Test Prevalence: 1,409 / 7,046 (20.0% positive, 80.0% negative).

2. Clinical Obesity (TARGET_OBESITY):
   - Definition: Measured Body Mass Index (BMI) >= 30.0 kg/m^2.
   - Train Prevalence: 8,932 / 23,694 (37.7% positive, 62.3% negative).
   - Test Prevalence: 3,075 / 7,046 (43.6% positive, 56.4% negative).

3. Cardiovascular Disease (TARGET_CVD):
   - Definition: Doctor-diagnosed Congestive Heart Failure, Coronary Heart 
     Disease, Angina Pectoris, Heart Attack (Myocardial Infarction), or Stroke.
   - Train Prevalence: 2,205 / 23,694 (9.3% positive, 90.7% negative).
   - Test Prevalence: 845 / 7,046 (12.0% positive, 88.0% negative).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 SUMMARY & CORE RATIONALE (LABEL PREVALENCE & IMBALANCE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Why Label 1s should NOT equal each other (Detailed Simple Breakdown):
   In our training data of 23,694 people:
     * Obesity (1s):  8,932 people (~37.5%)
     * Diabetes (1s): 3,022 people (~13.3%)
     * CVD (1s):      2,205 people (~9.9%)

   Why shouldn't these positive counts (1s) be forced to match?
   - Reason A (Real-World Biology): Diseases naturally occur at different rates. 
     In society, obesity is very common (~4 in 10 adults), diabetes is less common 
     (~1 in 7), and heart disease/stroke is the rarest (~1 in 10). They are not 
     supposed to be equal.
   - Reason B (Data Destruction): If you forced all diseases to match the lowest 
     count (CVD = 2,205), you would have to throw away 6,727 real obese patients:
       6,727 / 8,932 = ~73% of your obesity data deleted!
     Throwing away ~73% of real patients destroys the model's ability to learn 
     nutrition patterns, just to satisfy an artificial equality that doesn't exist.

2. Why 1s should NOT equal 0s (No artificial 50/50 balance):
   - Probability Calibration Collapse: Forcing a 50/50 balance makes the AI believe 
     any random human starts with a 50% baseline risk, artificially inflating predicted 
     risk outputs (e.g., predicting 60% risk instead of a realistic 10%).
   - Multi-Label Conflict: Because each patient has all 3 targets simultaneously, 
     cutting data to make CVD 50/50 destroys over 10,500 real patients (>79% of the 
     entire dataset) and corrupts Diabetes/Obesity learning.

3. How BioNutriGPT handles it mathematically:
   - Cost-sensitive weighted loss (pos_weight):
     * CVD missing a 1:      ~9x penalty (w_cvd = 9.74)
     * Diabetes missing a 1: ~6.5x penalty (w_diabetes = 6.84)
     * Obesity missing a 1:  ~1.7x penalty (w_obesity = 1.65)
   - Result: The AI is heavily penalized for missing rare positive cases without 
     distorting real-world prevalence percentages.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 MATHEMATICAL & ALGORITHMIC HANDLING IN BIONUTRIGPT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
If trained with standard unweighted binary cross-entropy, a deep neural network 
would exploit this class imbalance by predicting 0 for every patient, attaining a 
deceptively high 90.7% naive accuracy on CVD while failing completely as a 
clinical screening tool (0% sensitivity).

To prevent this pathological shortcut, BioNutriGPT implements three mathematical 
countermeasures:

1. Positive Class Weighting in Multi-Task Loss:
   - In the multi-task masked Binary Cross-Entropy loss, every positive error 
     is scaled by the positive imbalance factor w_t:
       w_t = min(N_negative_train / N_positive_train, 20.0)
   - Calculated Loss Weights:
       w_diabetes = 20,672 / 3,022 = 6.8405
       w_obesity  = 14,762 / 8,932 = 1.6527
       w_cvd      = 21,489 / 2,205 = 9.7456
   - Effect: Misclassifying a rare positive CVD patient as healthy incurs ~9.75x 
     higher gradient penalty than a false alarm, forcing the network to prioritize 
     atherogenic biomarker detection.

2. Decoupled Operating Thresholds (Youden's J Optimization):
   - Rather than relying on a naive 0.50 decision cutoff, optimal clinical 
     decision thresholds were tuned post-training by maximizing Youden's J Index 
     (Sensitivity + Specificity - 1):
       * Diabetes Operating Cutoff: 0.617
       * CVD Operating Cutoff:      0.603
       * Obesity Operating Cutoff:  0.445

3. Metric Independence from Base Rates:
   - Model validation and early stopping were governed by Area Under the ROC 
     Curve (AUROC) and Area Under the Precision-Recall Curve (PR-AUC) rather than 
     raw accuracy, ensuring optimization is invariant to the background 0/1 ratio.

4. NEURAL ARCHITECTURE DESIGN & HYPERPARAMETERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Script: train_bionutrigpt_stage1.py

- Layer 1: Feature Tokenizer (FT-Transformer Style)
  * Feature Dimension: 88 continuous/binary tokens.
  * Embedding Dimension (d_model): 128.
  * Per-Feature Projections: Each feature has its own dedicated learnable 
    weight W_j in R^128 and bias b_j in R^128:
      token_j = x_j * W_j + b_j  (Total Token Tensor: [B, 88, 128])
  * Modality Embeddings: Learnable modality identity embeddings M_bio, M_diet, 
    M_life, M_demo in R^128 added to prevent 46 dietary columns from 
    statistically overpowering 6 blood biomarkers.

- Layer 2: Cross-Modal Transformer Backbone
  * Depth: 3 stacked Transformer Encoder Layers.
  * Attention Heads: 4 heads (head_dim = 32).
  * Feedforward Dimension (dim_ff): 256.
  * Normalization: Pre-LayerNorm (norm_first = True) for gradient stability.
  * Activation: GELU (Gaussian Error Linear Unit).
  * Dropout: 0.10.
  * Function: Full 88 x 88 bidirectional cross-attention allowing blood tests 
    to attend directly to dietary sugars, sodium, and sitting minutes.

- Layer 3: Modality-Specific Attention Pooling
  * Replaces naive arithmetic averaging to prevent signal dilution.
  * Independent learnable query vectors v_bio, v_diet, v_life, v_demo in R^128.
  * Computes Softmax attention distribution across tokens in each modality slice.
  * Outputs 4 clean modality vectors: z_bio (128-d), z_diet (128-d), 
    z_life (128-d), z_demo (128-d). Total concatenated vector u in R^512.

- Layer 4: Sparse Adaptive Mixture of Experts (MoE)
  * Number of Experts: 4 specialized feedforward sub-networks.
  * Expert Architecture: Linear(512, 256) -> LayerNorm -> GELU -> Dropout -> Linear(256, 256).
  * Routing Mechanism: Top-2 Sparse Linear Gating with Softmax over top-2 logits.
  * Aux Load-Balancing Loss: Switch-Transformer entropy formulation prevents expert 
    collapse: L_balance = 4.0 * sum_{i=1}^4 (importance_i * load_i).
  * Output: Contextualized patient phenotype state z_patient in R^256.

- Layer 5: Clinical Residual Skip Highways & Multi-Task Heads
  * Diabetes Head: [z_patient (256-d) || z_bio (128-d)] in R^384 -> Linear(384, 64) -> GELU -> Linear(64, 1).
  * CVD Head:      [z_patient (256-d) || z_bio (128-d)] in R^384 -> Linear(384, 64) -> GELU -> Linear(64, 1).
  * Obesity Head:  [z_patient (256-d) || z_diet (128-d) || z_life (128-d)] in R^512 -> Linear(512, 64) -> GELU -> Linear(64, 1).
  * Direct residual injection ensures subtle laboratory abnormalities (e.g., HbA1c, 
    Non-HDL) never vanish through deep attention layers.

7. PHYSIOLOGICAL SENSITIVITY & COUNTERFACTUAL VERIFICATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To audit that Stage 1 learned true physiological rules rather than statistical 
shortcuts, we performed counterfactual perturbation tests on real patient vectors:

1. Glycemic Sensitivity Audit:
   - Increasing HbA1c from normal (5.2%) to diabetic (11.0%) on an identical patient 
     drives predicted Diabetes risk from 0.79% to 94.7% (+93.9 pp increase).
   - Validates that the clinical residual skip highway preserves glycemic control.

2. Phenotype MoE Routing Audit:
   - Healthy / Active Profile (HbA1c=5.2%, Non-HDL=122, Activity=1440 METs):
     * Routed to Expert 1 (79.84%) and Expert 0 (20.16%).
     * Inactive Experts: Expert 2 (0.0%), Expert 3 (0.0%).
   - Prediabetic / Atherogenic Profile (HbA1c=6.1%, Non-HDL=202, Sitting=600m):
     * Routed to Expert 2 (63.70%) and Expert 3 (36.30%).
     * Inactive Experts: Expert 0 (0.0%), Expert 1 (0.0%).
   - Severe Hypertensive Profile (Age 69, BP 186/102, Sodium 4567mg):
     * Routed to Expert 2 (73.75%) and Expert 3 (26.25%).
     * CVD Risk assigned: 90.36%.
   - Proves the sparse gating network dynamically segregates healthy vs. diseased 
     phenotypes without manual rule-crafting.

3. Modality Attention Allocation:
   - Attention Pooler weights consistently prioritize actionable features:
     * Bio Modality: Non-HDL (18-24%) and HbA1c (17-22%) capture the majority mass.
     * Diet Modality: Sodium, Sugars, Saturated Fat, and Potassium capture top attention.
     * Life Modality: Sedentary sitting time and smoking status dominate.
