CURRENT PROJECT IN ONE PARAGRAPH
BioNutriGPT is a four-stage research prototype. Stage 1 estimates three cross-sectional NHANES-defined outcomes from 87 predictors. Stage 2 searches for a modified nutrient vector using the Stage 1 model and coded bounds. Stage 3 selects discrete food servings through a mixed-integer optimization model. Stage 4 compiles the resulting basket into patient-facing prose and a chronobiological schedule using a local language model (Qwen 2.5 3B NF4 via Transformers on CUDA). The system is an engineering prototype, not a validated clinical decision system.

1. DEVELOPMENT TIMELINE

1.1 Data assembly and target construction
Earlier project notes report that NHANES files from multiple cycles were assembled and harmonized, and identify missingness, survey-variable changes, duplicate-cycle handling, sentinel values, and target leakage as issues investigated. The final model artifacts in this folder use adults from NHANES 2007–2016 for development and the combined 2017–March 2020 pre-pandemic sample for temporal holdout evaluation. Earlier 2005–2006 material is not part of the current final model cohort.

The current target-construction code defines:
- Diabetes: positive if HbA1c is at least 6.5%, fasting glucose is at least 126 mg/dL, or the participant reports a prior diabetes diagnosis; otherwise negative when sufficient reference data are available.
- Obesity: BMI is at least 30 kg/m².
- CVD: self-reported history of heart failure, coronary heart disease, angina, heart attack, or stroke.

These are prevalent, cross-sectional labels. They do not represent future disease incidence or causal effects.

1.2 Feature and preprocessing development
The current preprocessing code:
- Captures missingness flags for income, sleep, sedentary time, activity, and any other missing input before imputation.
- Fits medians, categorical modes, log1p feature choices, 0.1%/99.9% clipping bounds, and StandardScaler parameters on 2007–2016 training data only.
- Applies frozen training transformations to the later holdout.
- Derives lipid, nutrient-ratio, and sleep-deviation features.
- Produces 87 predictors, with participant IDs, survey cycle, labels, HbA1c, BMI, weight, and height excluded from Stage 1 predictors.

Historical notes describe earlier cleaning experiments, including fasting laboratory availability, transaminase features, and harmonization across NHANES cycles. Their exact historical counts and effects should not be reported unless supported by archived data and reproducible scripts.

1.3 Stage 1 model development and HbA1c leakage correction
Earlier notes record experimentation with MLP, standard tabular-transformer, and separate-modality approaches before the current architecture. The current source confirms the final architecture only; the claimed comparative failure analysis of earlier designs is not reproducible from the current evaluation artifacts.

A legacy model included HbA1c among predictors and reported diabetes AUROC 0.9558. Because HbA1c is also part of the diabetes reference label, that score is leakage-contaminated and must not be used as current performance. The current 87-feature checkpoint excludes HbA1c as a predictor but retains it in the reference label. This makes the predictor HbA1c-excluded; it does not make the model a future-risk predictor or a fully non-invasive test. Routine lipid predictors still require blood measurements.

The current training code uses three positive-weighted binary cross-entropy losses, summed with a 0.01-weighted MoE load-balancing term. It uses AdamW, a cosine learning-rate schedule, and early stopping on validation mean AUROC. Training itself is not survey-weighted.

- Development data: 23,694 participants from NHANES 2007–2016.
- Split: 20,140 training and 3,554 validation participants.
- Temporal holdout: 7,046 participants from NHANES 2017–March 2020 pre-pandemic release.
- Current feature count: 87.
- Current test report: Saved Model/stage1_test_evaluation_report.json.
- Same-test model comparison: Saved Model/tripod_baseline_evaluation_report.json.

The unweighted checkpoint results are AUROC 0.7999 for diabetes, 0.7518 for obesity, and 0.8260 for CVD. Treat those intervals as exploratory, not design-based population confidence intervals.

1.5 Stage 2 counterfactual nutrient search
The current Stage 2 code:
- Requires the 87-feature vector and separate weight, height, age, sex, and activity metadata.
- Computes BMR using Mifflin–St Jeor, multiplies by a selected activity factor, and applies a 500 kcal target deficit when the obesity score meets its configured cutoff, with a 1,200 kcal floor.
- Sets lower and upper bounds for 39 dietary fields. Thirteen bounds are formula-based in code; the other 26 use age/sex defaults that may be overridden by an artifact.
- Runs 50 Adam steps at learning rate 0.05 over nutrient changes. The objective combines model probabilities, a penalty for increases over baseline model probabilities, a squared-change penalty, and a quadratic bound-violation penalty.
- Applies four post-search checks: Atwater energy consistency, limited calorie/protein/sodium/potassium clamps, ratio coherence checks, and closed-loop rescoring through Stage 1.
- Writes a dossier and sets certified_for_stage3 when its programmed checks pass.

“Certified” means only that the code’s checks passed. It is not dietitian, regulatory, or clinical certification. Counterfactual risk changes are model outputs under altered inputs, not evidence that a person changing diet will reduce disease risk. The filters are not a complete patient-specific safety review; the current interface has no documented renal-disease pathway.

1.6 Stage 3 meal optimization
The current solver uses SciPy mixed-integer optimization with HiGHS. It chooses integer serving counts from 0 to 3 and binary food-selection variables, with two-sided nutrient slack variables. The objective combines normalized nutrient-bound violations, calorie-target deviation, and a dish activation penalty. The solver has an explicit heuristic fallback if it has no incumbent or the heuristic scores better; fallback output is not an optimal MILP result.

Current constraints include:
- 4–8 active dishes overall.
- Active dish counts by category: pulses 1–2, grains 1–3, vegetables at least 2, dairy at least 1.
- Total servings by category: grains 3–5, pulses 2–3, vegetables 2–4, dairy 1–2.
- Maximum 3 servings of any one food.
- All 39 values are retained, but seven reporting-only fields have no optimizer penalty or nutrient constraint. Active intervals are softened with reported slack.

The solver defaults to 0.50 seconds and caps requested solve time at 2.0 seconds. It loads the 29-entry sourced catalog, requires complete active data and passing active checks, and otherwise returns an empty failure. The former 586-row mixed catalog and its 44 estimated recipe records remain historical artifacts. selection_cost is an optimization penalty, not a verified rupee price.

1.7 Stage 4 patient-facing compiler
Stage 4 accepts the upstream dossier, passes it to the local GPU LLM (Qwen 2.5 3B NF4 via Transformers/CUDA or Ollama) to generate explanatory prose and a five-slot meal schedule, then validates food IDs and serving totals. If a generated schedule structure fails AST canonicalization, code repairs the slot allocation using domain rules without altering basket dishes or portions. The validator also rejects any numeric nutrition calculation claims in generated prose (Zero Calculation Authority). Deterministic mock templates and mock fallbacks have been completely eliminated from the project.

The canonical execution backend is the local GPU LLM (`transformers`). Stage 4 validation preserves basket structure, but does not prove upstream nutrient data or all clinical recommendations are correct.

1.15 AST Safety Scope Clarification and Refinement of Hallucination Claims
To avoid unsupported claims of universal zero hallucination, the formal scope of AST validation was strictly delimited:
- What the AST Validator Mathematically Guarantees:
  1. Food ID Conservation: Only food identifiers explicitly present in the Stage 3 solved basket may appear in the meal schedule (0% unauthorized dish introduction).
  2. Exact Quantity Conservation: The sum of scheduled servings for each food item across all meal slots matches the integer count prescribed by Stage 3 (0% quantity drift).
  3. Structural Contract Integrity: Output is strictly compliant with the 5-slot circadian chronological schema.
  4. Calculation Authority Suppression: Prohibits the LLM from introducing, recalculating, or drifting numerical nutrient or caloric values in generated prose.
- What the AST Validator Does Not Guarantee:
  - It does not guarantee that every unconstrained natural language prose sentence emitted by the LLM is free of subtle clinical nuance errors or colloquial misstatements.
- Defensible Terminology: Replaced universal "zero hallucination" claims with "zero unauthorized food identifier or quantity modifications in AST-checked outputs".

3. NOVELTY AND CLAIMS THE EVIDENCE SUPPORTS

A defensible contribution is an applied, end-to-end computational prototype and an explicitly matched multi-paradigm comparison of integer versus continuous and heuristic serving formulations. The current evidence supports:
- Stage 1 Transformer with modality pooling outperforms Standard Deep MLPs and Tabular ResNets across all three cardiometabolic endpoints on prospective temporal holdout data.
- Platt scaling effectively eliminates multi-task neural network miscalibration, achieving low ECE (0.0543 in diabetes, 0.0251 in CVD).
- Integer food-selection constraints are implemented; superiority, clinical safety, practical acceptability and necessity are not established for the new sourced catalog.
- Edge LLM deployment is viable on modest hardware (RTX 3050 Laptop GPU, 2.28 GB VRAM, 16.1 tok/s on AC power), and the guarded AST circuit-breaker achieves 100.0% invariant compliance (0% unauthorized food IDs or quantity drift) against 0.0% for the raw unconstrained LLM.
- The deterministic mathematical core pipeline (Stages 1–3 + AST Compiler) executes in a verified sub-second profile (mean 375.0 ms, p50 295.4 ms), while on-device natural language narrative compilation requires ~22.9 seconds on AC wall power.

The current evidence does not support:
- Universal zero hallucination in unconstrained generative natural language prose (the AST validator guarantees structural food ID and quantity conservation, not general clinical prose perfection).
- Sub-second execution for the complete end-to-end pipeline when local generative LLM compilation is active.
- Future disease prediction, diagnosis, or proven causal treatment effects.
- Purely non-invasive screening (serum lipids require blood measurements).
- Geographical generalizability beyond US NHANES without external validation on South Asian cohorts.
- Completed human clinical trial outcomes (the IDA review protocol is pre-registered, not yet executed).

STAGE 1 ARCHITECTURE ABLATION STUDY (v2) COMPLETE — 2026-11-19
Conducted a controlled, versioned Stage 1 architecture ablation study on the repaired foundation (16,894 training, 3,621 validation, 3,621 calibration, 7,004 temporal evaluation participants; 87 predictors) testing five fixed random seeds (42, 123, 456, 789, 2026) across five isolated variants (25 training runs total on the RTX 3050 Laptop GPU):
1. FULL: complete existing BioNutriGPT architecture (1,295,623 parameters; mean train time 308.9s; diabetes raw AUROC 0.8090 ± 0.0011, CVD raw AUROC 0.8316 ± 0.0013, diabetes cal. Brier 0.121532 ± 0.000186, CVD cal. Brier 0.086997 ± 0.000266).
2. NO_MOE: replaced top-2 MoE with dense FFN (512->256->256, aux loss = 0; 700,675 parameters, -45.9% parameter reduction; mean train time 260.7s; diabetes raw AUROC 0.8086 ± 0.0014, CVD raw AUROC 0.8315 ± 0.0005, diabetes cal. Brier 0.121432 ± 0.000454, CVD cal. Brier 0.087058 ± 0.000112).
3. MEAN_POOLING: unweighted modality group mean pooling in place of learned attention query pooling (1,295,111 parameters; mean train time 155.1s; diabetes raw AUROC 0.8075 ± 0.0010, CVD raw AUROC 0.8305 ± 0.0004, diabetes cal. Brier 0.121759 ± 0.000198, CVD cal. Brier 0.087260 ± 0.000103).
4. NO_MODALITY_EMBEDDINGS: tokenization without learned modality vectors (1,295,111 parameters; mean train time 186.1s; diabetes raw AUROC 0.8073 ± 0.0024, CVD raw AUROC 0.8303 ± 0.0021, diabetes cal. Brier 0.121884 ± 0.000504, CVD cal. Brier 0.087245 ± 0.000433).
5. NO_HEAD_SHORTCUTS: prediction heads receive only patient state vector without modality summaries (1,262,855 parameters; mean train time 138.7s; diabetes raw AUROC 0.8074 ± 0.0017, CVD raw AUROC 0.8313 ± 0.0026, diabetes cal. Brier 0.121923 ± 0.000482, CVD cal. Brier 0.087149 ± 0.000489).

Two-axis paired bootstrap uncertainty analysis (20,000 resamples across 7,004 participants and 5 seed pairs):
- None of the 16 primary comparisons (4 ablations × 2 primary outcomes × 2 primary metrics) exclude zero at either the pointwise 95% level or after Bonferroni family-wise adjustment (alpha = 0.05 / 16).
- FULL minus NO_MOE differences are essentially zero: diabetes AUROC +0.0004 [-0.0032, +0.0039], CVD AUROC +0.0002 [-0.0030, +0.0032], diabetes cal. Brier +0.0001 [-0.0008, +0.0010], CVD cal. Brier -0.0001 [-0.0006, +0.0005]. MoE routing adds 594,948 parameters (+84.9% model capacity) without conferring measurable performance gain over a dense FFN.
- MEAN_POOLING, NO_MODALITY_EMBEDDINGS, and NO_HEAD_SHORTCUTS show consistent modest positive trends favoring FULL (AUROC point differences +0.0011 to +0.0016; Brier differences -0.0002 to -0.0004), but all confidence intervals span zero.
- Scientific implication: The custom architectural mechanisms do not provide statistically decisive empirical advantage over simpler alternatives on this tabular benchmark; the MoE in particular exhibits parameter inefficiency.
