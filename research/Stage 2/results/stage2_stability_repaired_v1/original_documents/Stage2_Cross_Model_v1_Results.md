## 5. Main Engineering Conclusions

1. **Cross-Model Agreement is Strong for Obesity and CVD:**
   - **Obesity:** High cross-model consistency across architectures. All three models assign large, statistically distinguishable risk score reductions on reference proposals (mean $\Delta p$: BioNutriGPT $-0.0414$, CatBoost $-0.0317$, FT-Transformer $-0.0345$, with $\ge 81\%$ of participants assigned decreases in all models).
   - **Cardiovascular Disease:** Robust cross-model agreement. All three models evaluate reference proposals as achieving lower risk scores (mean $\Delta p$: BioNutriGPT $-0.0068$, CatBoost $-0.0051$, FT-Transformer $-0.0083$, with $\ge 66\%$ of participants assigned decreases).
2. **Cross-Model Divergence on Diabetes:**
   - In the unstratified 100-cohort evaluation, BioNutriGPT assigns diabetes score reductions ($-0.0098$), FT-Transformer evaluates them as neutral ($-0.0024$, 95% CI $[-0.0113, +0.0067]$ spanning zero), and CatBoost assigns an average score increase ($+0.0132$, 95% CI $[+0.0061, +0.0207]$).
   - In the source-accepted stratum ($N=62$), CatBoost's diabetes score increase shrinks toward neutrality ($+0.0031$, $[-0.0044, +0.0113]$), while FT-Transformer's score reduction strengthens to $-0.0139$ ($[-0.0238, -0.0047]$).
3. **Model Guidance Adds Cross-Architecture Value Over Unguided Search:**
   - Comparing the full reference variant against the unguided variant (`variant_0` minus `variant_1`) demonstrates that Stage 2 model guidance consistently shifts nutrient targets into regions scored as lower risk by the comparator models:
     - In FT-Transformer, model guidance reduces risk scores across **all three endpoints** (diabetes $-0.0150$, obesity $-0.0040$, CVD $-0.0071$).
     - In CatBoost, model guidance reduces diabetes risk relative to unguided search ($-0.0021$, $[-0.0036, -0.0007]$) and produces negligible differences for obesity ($+0.0003$) and CVD ($-0.0000$).
   - Optimization does not merely "game" idiosyncratic artifacts of BioNutriGPT; its directional improvements are partly or fully recognized by independent tabular architectures.

## 6. Disclosures, Limitations & Scope Boundaries

1. **Shared Observational Training Data:** CatBoost, FT-Transformer, and BioNutriGPT were trained and calibrated on the same underlying observational NHANES cohort. While architectural implementations are strictly independent, agreement between them does not represent independent-cohort validation or external prospective replication.
2. **Observational Predictions $\ne$ Clinical Intervention Effects:** Predicted probability shifts reflect associative functions fitted on observational survey data. They do not demonstrate causal intervention efficacy, patient safety, or therapeutic effectiveness.
3. **Off-Manifold Inputs:** Optimization proposals can generate combinations of nutrients near the boundary of the observational training manifold, where model response surfaces may extrapolate inconsistently.
4. **Obesity / BMI Linkage:** BMI is a strong predictive feature for all three endpoints. Because Stage 2 optimizes nutrient features without modifying baseline anthropometrics, predicted obesity probability changes reflect diet-only coefficients.
5. **No Production Modifications:** Production code, Stage 1 weights, preprocessors, and Stage 2 counterfactual targets remain 100% untouched.
