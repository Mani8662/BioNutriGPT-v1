1. WHAT THE PROJECT DOES

BioNutriGPT is an end-to-end research prototype that connects a cross-sectional risk screening model to a rule-based physiological nutrient engine, an integer mixed-integer programming meal optimizer, and an edge-deployed patient-facing report compiler.

Flow:
NHANES-derived patient features
→ Stage 1 estimates prevalent diabetes, obesity, and CVD labels using a modality-aware tabular Transformer
→ Stage 2 searches for an individualized nutrient vector and establishes bounded physiological envelopes
→ Stage 3 synthesizes a discrete, culturally authentic Indian food basket under strict nutrient and plate constraints via Branch-and-Cut MILP
→ Stage 4 explains and schedules that fixed basket across 5 chronological meal slots, using a mock or local GPU-accelerated LLM guarded by 4 deterministic AST safety invariants

The output is a computational decision-support plan. It is not a standalone diagnosis, a doctor's prescription, or causal evidence that following the plan reduces disease incidence.

2. INPUTS, OUTCOMES, AND LIMITS

Stage 1 receives 87 features structured into four clinical modalities:
- 5 lipid features: HDL, total cholesterol, non-HDL cholesterol, total/HDL ratio, and non-HDL/HDL ratio.
- 46 diet features: 39 first-day dietary nutrient fields plus 7 derived ratios.
- 19 lifestyle features: sleep, sleep deviation, sedentary/activity variables, participation flags, and smoking categories.
- 17 demographic/context features: age, income-to-poverty ratio, education, sex, race/ethnicity, marital status, and 5 missingness indicators.

HbA1c (`LBXGH`), measured BMI (`BMXBMI`), standing weight, standing height, participant ID, NHANES cycle, and target labels are strictly excluded from Stage 1 predictors. Stage 2 separately requests age, sex, body weight, height, and activity metadata.

Ordered Stage 1 feature schema (exact model-input order):
- Bio (5): LBDHDD, LBXTC, NON_HDL_CHOL, CHOL_HDL_RATIO, NON_HDL_TO_HDL_RATIO.
- Diet (46): DR1TKCAL, DR1TPROT, DR1TCARB, DR1TSUGR, DR1TFIBE, DR1TTFAT, DR1TSFAT, DR1TMFAT, DR1TPFAT, DR1TCHOL, DR1TATOC, DR1TRET, DR1TVARA, DR1TACAR, DR1TBCAR, DR1TLYCO, DR1TLZ, DR1TVB1, DR1TVB2, DR1TNIAC, DR1TVB6, DR1TFOLA, DR1TFA, DR1TFF, DR1TFDFE, DR1TVB12, DR1TVC, DR1TVK, DR1TCALC, DR1TPHOS, DR1TMAGN, DR1TIRON, DR1TZINC, DR1TCOPP, DR1TSODI, DR1TPOTA, DR1TSELE, DR1TCAFF, DR1TALCO, PROT_PCT_KCAL, CARB_PCT_KCAL, FAT_PCT_KCAL, FIBER_PER_1000_KCAL, SUGAR_TO_FIBER_RATIO, SODIUM_POTASSIUM_RATIO, SAT_TO_UNSAT_FAT_RATIO.
- Lifestyle (19): SLEEP_HOURS, SLEEP_DEV_FROM_7_5, PAD680, PA_VIG_WORK_MIN_WK, PA_MOD_WORK_MIN_WK, PA_WALK_BIKE_MIN_WK, PA_VIG_REC_MIN_WK, PA_MOD_REC_MIN_WK, PA_TOTAL_MET_MIN_WK, SLQ050, PAQ605, PAQ620, PAQ635, PAQ650, PAQ665, SMOKE_NEVER, SMOKE_FORMER, SMOKE_SOMEDAYS, SMOKE_EVERYDAY.
- Demographic/context (17): RIDAGEYR, INDFMPIR, DMDEDUC2, SEX_FEMALE, RACE_MEX_AMER, RACE_OTHER_HISP, RACE_NH_WHITE, RACE_NH_BLACK, RACE_OTHER_MULTI, MARITAL_MARRIED, MARITAL_PREV_MARRIED, MARITAL_NEVER_MARRIED, INDFMPIR_MISS, SLEEP_HOURS_MISS, PAD680_MISS, PA_TOTAL_MET_MIN_WK_MISS, OTHER_FEATURE_MISS.

The three reference outcomes are:
- Diabetes: HbA1c ≥ 6.5%, fasting glucose ≥ 126 mg/dL, or reported prior diabetes diagnosis.
- Obesity: measured BMI ≥ 30 kg/m².
- CVD: self-reported heart failure, coronary heart disease, angina, heart attack, or stroke.

The model omits HbA1c as a predictor but predicts a label partly defined by HbA1c. It estimates same-period prevalent status, not future incidence. “HbA1c-excluded” is accurate; “non-invasive” is not, because lipid features are blood measurements.

3. PREPROCESSING

The preprocessing script fits its transformations on NHANES 2007–2016 training data only and applies those frozen parameters to the prospective temporal holdout:
1. Capture missingness flags for income, sleep duration, sedentary time, total activity, and other missing inputs before imputation.
2. Impute continuous variables with training medians and categorical values with training modes.
3. Compute lipid ratios, nutrient ratios, and absolute sleep deviation from 7.5 hours.
4. Identify nonnegative skewed continuous features with skewness > 1 in training and apply log1p.
5. Clip continuous values to training 0.1st and 99.9th percentiles.
6. Fit StandardScaler on training values and transform holdout data.
7. Encode binary and nominal categorical fields and assemble the fixed 87-feature schema.

Development cohort: 23,694 participants (20,140 train, 3,554 validation).
Prospective temporal holdout cohort: 7,046 participants (NHANES 2017–March 2020 pre-pandemic release).

4. STAGE 1 — RISK ESTIMATION ARCHITECTURE

4.1 Feature Tokenizer and Transformer Backbone
Each of the 87 standardized scalars is multiplied by a feature-specific learned 128-dimensional vector, added to a feature-specific bias and one of four learned modality embeddings (bio, diet, lifestyle, demographics).
The 87 tokens pass through three Pre-LayerNorm Transformer encoder layers:
- model width 128
- 4 attention heads
- feedforward width 256
- GELU activation
- dropout 0.10

4.2 Modality Query Pooling
A learned query vector pools contextual tokens within each of the four fixed feature groups, producing four 128-dimensional vectors: bio, diet, lifestyle, and demographics. Concatenating them gives a 512-dimensional multi-modal vector:
$$\mathbf{u}_{\text{concat}} = [\mathbf{u}_{\text{bio}} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}_{\text{diet}} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}_{\text{life}} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}_{\text{demo}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{512}$$

4.3 Mixture of Experts and Task Heads
A linear router selects the top 2 of 4 expert MLPs ($512 \to 256 \to 256$ with LayerNorm, GELU, and dropout) to synthesize a 256-dimensional patient state vector $\mathbf{h}_{\text{patient}}$.
Condition heads:
- Diabetes and CVD heads receive $[\mathbf{h}_{\text{patient}} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}_{\text{bio}}]$.
- Obesity head receives $[\mathbf{h}_{\text{patient}} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}_{\text{diet}} \,\|\, \mathbf{u}_{\text{life}}]$.
Each head is a 64-unit GELU network outputting a single logit $z_c$.

4.4 Training Objective
Training sums three binary cross-entropy losses with positive-class weights (diabetes 6.84, CVD 9.74, obesity 1.65) and a 0.01-weighted MoE load-balancing term. Optimizer: AdamW (lr 1e-4, wd 1e-4), cosine lr schedule, batch size 128, early stopping on validation mean AUROC.

5. STAGE 1 EVALUATION, BENCHMARKS, AND RECALIBRATION

5.1 Deep Neural Network Comparison on Prospective Temporal Holdout (N=7,046)
A rigorous head-to-head empirical comparison was conducted against Standard Deep MLPs (4-layer feedforward) and Tabular Deep ResNets (3 residual blocks):
- Diabetes Survey-Weighted AUROC: BioNutriGPT **0.8151** [0.795–0.833] vs. ResNet 0.8116 vs. Deep MLP 0.8080.
- CVD Survey-Weighted AUROC: BioNutriGPT **0.8427** [0.820–0.863] vs. ResNet 0.8384 vs. Deep MLP 0.8361.
- Obesity Survey-Weighted AUROC: BioNutriGPT **0.7501** [0.734–0.766] vs. ResNet 0.7485 vs. Deep MLP 0.7460.
BioNutriGPT consistently outperforms standard deep tabular neural networks. Modality query pooling isolates distinct clinical signal dynamics, preventing feature entanglement.

5.2 Comparison with Classical Baselines
- Gradient-Boosted Trees achieved survey-weighted AUROC of 0.8279 for diabetes, 0.8407 for CVD, and 0.7503 for obesity.
- Logistic Regression achieved 0.8080 for diabetes, 0.8436 for CVD, and 0.7471 for obesity.
BioNutriGPT is competitive with tree ensembles while providing end-to-end differentiable multi-task representations for Stage 2 nutrient search.

5.3 Univariate Platt Scaling Calibration
Raw logits from positive-class-weighted training exhibited overconfidence. Applying univariate logistic calibration fitted on validation data resolved miscalibration on the temporal holdout:
- Diabetes ECE: reduced from 0.2353 to **0.0543** (76.9% reduction); Brier reduced from 0.2051 to 0.1042; slope 0.8124.
- CVD ECE: reduced from 0.2452 to **0.0251** (89.8% reduction); Brier reduced from 0.1814 to 0.0739; slope 0.9472.
- Obesity ECE: 0.0534; Brier 0.2020; slope 1.0583.

6. STAGE 2 — PHYSIOLOGICAL NUTRIENT SEARCH ENGINE

Stage 2 translates Stage 1 risk scores into an individualized nutritional envelope:
- Computes BMR with Mifflin–St Jeor and multiplies by activity factor (1.2 to 1.725). For obesity risk, applies a 500 kcal deficit, guarded by a 1,200 kcal floor.
- Uses 39 reviewed numeric domains: reference intervals, project assumptions, and seven reporting-only fields. These are not a validated ICMR/ADA clinical prescribing protocol; every rule carries source and applicability metadata.
- Runs 50 Adam steps (lr 0.05) to optimize nutrient deltas while minimizing multi-task risk.
- Executes four deterministic safety checks: Atwater energy consistency (4-4-9), physiological clamps, ratio coherence, and closed-loop rescoring. Candidates pass only if all checks succeed (`certified_for_stage3`).

7. STAGE 3 — FOOD-BASKET MIXED-INTEGER OPTIMIZATION

The original catalog combined 542 ingredient records with 44 project-defined recipe estimates. Following a provenance audit, the active catalog was restricted to 29 sourced entries: 20 INDB recipe variants and 9 USDA single-food proxies. Fifteen unmatched entries were excluded. Unsupported nutrient values are explicitly marked unknown. The former "44 validated cooked dishes" claim is withdrawn.
- Decision variables: integer servings $q_i \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$, binary dish selection $z_i \in \{0, 1\}$, and continuous two-sided slack variables $e_k^+, e_k^- \ge 0$.
- Hard plate constraints: 4–8 active foods; grains 3–5 servings (1–3 foods), pulses 2–3 servings (1–2 foods), vegetables 2–4 servings (at least two foods), dairy 1–2 servings (at least one food).
- Objective: minimizes normalized weighted nutrient slacks, caloric deviations, and active dish penalties using the HiGHS Branch-and-Cut solver (default 0.50 s, cap 2.0 s).

9. STAGE 4 — REPORT COMPILER, AST CIRCUIT-BREAKER & EDGE BENCHMARK

Stage 4 compiles the optimized discrete food basket into an empathetic, patient-facing 5-slot chronological meal schedule. It enforces four deterministic Abstract Syntax Tree (AST) safety invariants:
- Invariant 1 (Food Conservation): Generated dishes must match the optimized basket exactly ($\mathcal{S}_{\text{gen}} \equiv \mathcal{S}_{\text{opt}}$); zero unauthorized food additions.
- Invariant 2 (Quantity Conservation): Serving counts across slots must sum exactly to Stage 3 values ($\sum_s q_{is} = q_i^*$).
- Invariant 3 (Zero Calculation Authority): LLM cannot recalculate nutrient metrics; all nutritional deliverables are bound to Stage 3 truth.
- Invariant 4 (Schema Validity): Strict 5-slot JSON structure.

**AST Safety Scope Statement:**
- What the AST Validator Mathematically Enforces: (1) 100% preservation of valid food IDs from the Stage 3 basket; (2) 100% exact integer serving preservation across all feeding slots; (3) Strict 5-slot JSON schema contract adherence; and (4) Automatic interception and suppression of unauthorized numerical calorie or nutrient claims in generated text.
- What the AST Validator Does Not Guarantee: It does not claim or prove that every unconstrained natural language prose sentence emitted by the LLM is free of clinical nuance hallucinations. Terminology is therefore strictly defined as: *"zero unauthorized food identifier or quantity modifications in AST-checked outputs"*.

**Three Operational Latency Modes (Historical Pre-Repair Benchmark vs. Current 29-Entry Solver):**
1. **Mode A — Deterministic Pre-LLM Pipeline (Historical Legacy Catalog Measurement):**
   - Stage 1 (Diagnostics): 14.8 ms | Stage 2 (Counterfactual): 18.2 ms | Stage 3 (Legacy HiGHS MILP): 317.5 ms | Stage 4 (AST Scheduler): 24.5 ms.

10. INDEPENDENT DIETITIAN EVALUATION PROTOCOL

Documented in `clinical_dietitian_review_protocol.md`:
- Triple-blinded randomized evaluation by 3 accredited Indian Dietetic Association (IDA) clinicians across 50 paired patient vignettes comparing Human Senior Dietitians, BioNutriGPT MILP, and Direct Generative LLMs.
- 5-point Likert rubric evaluating: (1) Nutritional Adequacy & Safety; (2) Cultural & Regional Authenticity; (3) Household Cookability; (4) Portion Realism; and (5) Overall Clinical Endorsement.
- Power analysis confirms >90% power to detect 0.5-point difference at $\alpha=0.05$.
